Your language deserves a dub too.

Estonian, Latvian, Slovenian, Maltese: many of Europe's languages rarely get a dub. DubAI FanSUB turns a series episode into a finished fan dub. One Mac prepares the episode: it separates the voices from the music and sorts every line by character. The cast records from home in any web browser, and AI voices that run on the Mac can speak the lines nobody has recorded yet.

Dubbing used to need a studio. Most of that work can happen at home.

Big markets get official dubs. Smaller language communities often get subtitles only, because a studio dub needs booked rooms, an engineer and a clean music-and-effects track that never reaches them. DubAI FanSUB rebuilds each of those pieces from the episode itself.

AI voices that run on your Mac, and stay editable after they speak

Every model DubAI FanSUB uses runs on the host's Mac. Nothing is uploaded to a voice service, there is no subscription and no per-minute price. Generated lines arrive as ordinary takes, so you listen, fix and approve them exactly like a recording from a voice actor.

A voice for every character

Open a character and give them a voice. It belongs to the series, so it carries over to the next episode.

  • Clone from the episode. FanSUB finds a clean 3–10 second passage where only this character speaks, or you pick a section yourself.
  • Import a recording. Any audio or video: music and effects are taken out, the right speaker is found and cut to the best part.
  • Design one. Describe the voice, hear a sample and keep the one you like.

Generate every line in their voice

Choose the characters, pick Best or Faster, and the whole episode is spoken in the target language, each line fitted to the time the original took.

  • Keep the humans. Lines that already have a recorded take are left alone, so actors and AI voices share one cast.
  • Fitted to the scene. A line may speed up a little to fit its slot, with the pitch kept; the video is never retimed.
  • Only what changed. Unchanged lines are not generated again, so a second run takes seconds.

Fix and edit after generation

A generated take is a starting point, not a final answer. Everything that can be done to a recorded take can be done to a generated one.

  • Lines that don't fit are listed with how long they need and how long they have. Give one a shorter alternative or more time, then generate again.
  • Timing guide. Say the line yourself, or import a recording, and the generated voice follows your pauses and rhythm, not your voice.
  • Take editor. Split, move and trim parts on the timeline, change speed, pitch and level, undo anything.
  • With the picture and every word. Open the scene’s video above the editor, and its timeline shows 10 seconds with every word of the line. Click a word and the cut lands exactly between words.
  • Checked for you. Each take is listened to again: wrong words, the wrong voice or a bad fit is flagged, and the rest can be approved together.

Watch it before you release it

The Theatre plays the episode with the fandub: everyone together, or one character at a time to hear all their lines in a row. The bar under it shows which lines are approved, waiting or still missing.

Need a clip for the group chat? Export any stretch of the episode with the dub as an MP4 or MKV.

Always know where you are

A dub takes evenings, not one sitting. The Dashboard opens on your second display and keeps the whole episode in view while you work on the first.

  • Next up. The first part with something left to do, and a button that takes you there.
  • Where you were. The screen, line and moment you stopped at last time. One click and you continue there.
  • Every part at a glance. Done, to do or optional, with progress and what’s left: “90 lines need a character”, “11 takes to review”.
  • Latest changes. Lines assigned and checked, takes arriving, approved or edited, voices generated, grouped by day.
  • New takes announced. When an actor sends a take, you get a notification, a banner or a count on the Dock icon, wherever you are in the app.

Password and approval

A session needs its password. Five wrong tries lock a device out for a minute, and one click ends the session for everyone.

Three ways to connect

Plain HTTP on a home network you trust, HTTPS with a certificate the app can make, or behind a reverse proxy such as Caddy or nginx for actors further away.

Nothing left behind

Lines an actor keeps for offline practice stay in their own browser, and one button removes them. Taken characters only show the actor's chosen name.

Voices with consent

Clone or import a voice only with the speaker's permission. A group member's own recording is the easiest way to give a side character a real voice.

Any language you can speak

When people record, the language is whatever your group speaks, and that is the point: languages with a few million speakers or fewer rarely get an official dub, but they have fansub groups, drama clubs and families who would make one. The translation is the subtitle your fansub team already writes, and if the release carries a subtitle in the original language or in English, FanSUB uses that text for syncing.

AI voices speak the same subtitles in the target language. The voice model knows over 600 languages, so smaller ones are covered too; how natural it sounds varies by language, which is why every generated line goes through the Takes inbox.

DubAI GameDUB

New · A second app, for games

Games deserve a dub too. GameDUB gets every line ready.

Most games ship with voices in a handful of languages and subtitles for the rest. GameDUB is FanSUB's sister app for game translation and modding teams: point it at a game's folder and it finds the original voice files and the text tables, puts every spoken line together, and times where each word is said, so a dub can match the original.

Drop in a game's folder

Choose the folder, or the .app, or drop it on the Dock icon. GameDUB tells you what it found and what still needs unpacking.

  • It knows the engine. Unity, Unreal Engine, Godot, Ren'Py, RPG Maker MV/MZ and GameMaker, and Wwise or FMOD inside them, each read the way that engine writes its files.
  • Voices inside sound banks. Voices in Wwise and FMOD banks become lines and are taken out with one button. Music and effects stay out.
  • Converted for you. Ogg, Opus, xWMA, Wwise and Xbox audio are converted to WAV with ffmpeg or vgmstream, so every line plays and can be transcribed.
  • Scan again, keep the work. After you unpack an archive or the game updates, a new scan keeps timings, dub text and characters.

Every line, with its voice and its speaker

Text and recordings are matched up: the game's ID, who says it, the text and the original voice file, in one table you can search and filter.

  • Matched by name or by script. A dialogue script's voice link wins, then the file's path or key, compared without case or punctuation.
  • Speakers found for you. From a speaker column, a Ren'Py Character, an RPG Maker name box, or the voice folder (VO/en/guard/… → Guard).
  • Menus left out. Item names and tips with no voice and no speaker are counted, not listed as dialogue.

Know when every word is said

Each voice file is transcribed on the Mac with Apple's SpeechAnalyzer. That gives a dub the timing it has to hit.

  • Speech start and end. The silence before and after, the pauses, and how fast the line is spoken.
  • The game's text, word by word. Each word of the script is matched to the moment it was heard, and words the actor changed or skipped are marked.
  • Voice-only lines get text. Barks with no subtitle get their words from the transcription.

Write a dub that fits

The Script screen puts the dub's text beside the original, with the time each line has.

  • Will it fit? Each translation gets an estimate of how long it takes at the original's pace, marked as fitting, short, or how much too long it is.
  • The game's limits. A length limit per line, for subtitles and UI that can only hold so many characters.
  • A cast for the game. Characters with their lines, words and seconds of speech, a colour and the voice actor who dubs them.

Dub responsibly

Make fandubs only of releases you are allowed to use, and follow the rules of the rights holders and your community. Clone a voice only with its owner's consent. DubAI FanSUB never changes the source file; everything it makes is saved next to it as separate tracks, so the original always stays intact.
